### Question 1: Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and the "Unfolding Argument"
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Giulio Tononi (2004, 2016), Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger & Michael Herzog (2019)

In Integrated Information Theory (IIT 3.0 / 4.0), Giulio Tononi, Christof Koch, and Larissa Albantakis propose that consciousness is an intrinsic, fundamental property of physical systems, quantitatively measured by the metric $\Phi^{\text{max}}$ (integrated information across the system's minimum informational partition). According to IIT, recurrent feedback connections generate high $\Phi$, while purely feedforward systems have $\Phi = 0$ (completely non-conscious). 

In their celebrated paper "The Unfolding Argument" (2019), Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, and Michael Herzog formulate a mathematical and philosophical refutation of IIT's claim to empirically measure consciousness. What is the core structure of the **Unfolding Argument**?

- (A) They show that the value of $\Phi$ becomes complex (containing imaginary numbers $i$) when applied to quantum entangled qubits in the cerebellum.
- (B) They demonstrate that feedforward networks require infinite spatial dimensions to compute Boolean disjunctions, violating special relativity.
- (C) They prove that because information is conserved under Shannon entropy, any system with $\Phi > 0$ violates the second law of thermodynamics.
- (D) By standard theorems in computational complexity and automata theory, any recurrent neural network with feedback can be mathematically "unfolded" into an input-output equivalent feedforward network; since causal structure theories like IIT assign $\Phi > 0$ to the recurrent net and $\Phi = 0$ to the unfolded feedforward net, IIT either makes untestable metaphysical claims detached from empirical input-output behavior, or it arbitrarily declares one computationally identical system conscious and the other an unconscious zombie.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Integrated Information Theory (IIT) makes a radical metaphysical departure from functionalism: IIT claims that consciousness is not determined by what a system *does* (its input-output computational function), but by its internal **causal architecture** (cause-effect structure). According to IIT:
- A recurrent network with dense feedback loops can possess high $\Phi^{\text{max}}$ (rich phenomenal consciousness).
- An input-output equivalent feedforward network (lacking recurrent loops) has $\Phi^{\text{max}} = 0$ (a complete, non-conscious philosophical zombie).

In "The Unfolding Argument: Why Causal Theories of Consciousness Cannot Be Empirically Tested" (2019), Doerig, Schurger, and Herzog demonstrate that this creates a fatal epistemic crisis for IIT:
1. *The Mathematical Fact*: It is a fundamental mathematical theorem of computer science that for any finite-time recurrent neural network (RNN), there exists an "unfolded" purely feedforward neural network (FFN) that produces the *exact same input-output function*.
2. *The Epistemic Dilemma*: When conducting an empirical experiment on consciousness, science can only measure a subject’s inputs (stimuli) and outputs (verbal reports, button presses, behavioral responses):
   - If IIT claims the recurrent net is conscious while the unfolded feedforward net is an unconscious zombie despite identical reports, IIT's claims are **empirically unfalsifiable**, because no behavioral, reportable, or functional test can ever distinguish between them.
   - If IIT accepts that they are both conscious or both unconscious, then IIT’s core postulate—that recurrent causal structure is necessary for consciousness—collapses.

Thus, the Unfolding Argument shows that causal-structure theories like IIT fall into a radical form of untestable epiphenomenalism.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: $\Phi$ is a non-negative real scalar ($\Phi \ge 0$); it never produces imaginary numbers, and the Unfolding Argument is classical, not quantum-mechanical.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Feedforward networks operate in ordinary finite Euclidean space and compute standard Boolean logic without violating relativity.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Shannon entropy and the Second Law of Thermodynamics are not violated; IIT utilizes mathematical concepts from information theory, but the Unfolding Argument is about input-output equivalence in computational networks, not thermodynamic entropy.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, Kathryn Hess, and Michael H. Herzog, "The Unfolding Argument: Why Causal Theories of Consciousness Cannot Be Empirically Tested", *Consciousness and Cognition* 72 (2019): 49–59; Giulio Tononi, "Integrated Information Theory: From Consciousness to Its Physical Substrate", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 17(7) (2016): 450–461.

### Question 2: Panpsychism and the Triple Combination Problem
**Format**: Multi-Statement Evaluation / MSQ (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: William James (1890), David J. Chalmers (2016), Philip Goff (2017)

In *The Principles of Psychology* (1890), William James formulated the classic objection to mind-dust theory: *"Take a hundred of them, each sensing neither more nor less than its own single constituent, and pack them as closely as you will; still, you cannot make an aggregate sensation of the hundred out of them."* 

In contemporary panpsychism, David Chalmers (2016) systematicizes James’s objection into the **Triple Combination Problem** facing constitutive micro-panpsychism. Consider the following three sub-problems:
1. *The Subject Combination Problem*: How do myriad micro-subjects of experience (e.g., millions of micro-conscious quarks and leptons) combine to constitute a single, unified, macro-subject of experience (such as a human "I")?
2. *The Quality Combination Problem*: How do micro-qualities (the primitive proto-phenomenal or phenomenal micro-properties of fundamental physics) combine to yield the radically distinct, rich macro-qualities of human phenomenology (such as the phenomenal redness of a burgundy wine or the distinct taste of dark chocolate)?
3. *The Structural Mismatch Problem*: How can the concrete, spatio-temporally distributed mathematical-physical structure of micro-particles in the brain match the unified, non-aggregated, phenomenological structure of the macro-conscious visual field?

Which of the following evaluations of these three sub-problems are strictly philosophically accurate?
- (A) Panpsychists resolve Statement 1 by proving that micro-subjects fuse through quantum decoherence into a single macro-boson.
- (B) Statements 1, 2, and 3 are all authentic, distinct sub-problems of the Combination Problem as formalized by Chalmers, and panpsychists like Philip Goff adopt Cosmopsychism precisely to invert the Subject Combination Problem.
- (C) Only Statement 1 is an actual philosophical problem; Statements 2 and 3 are merely empirical questions resolved by Fourier transform analysis.
- (D) The Subject Combination Problem applies only to Cartesian substance dualism and is logically dissolved under any materialist ontology.

**Correct Answer**: **(B)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
David Chalmers’s landmark paper "The Combination Problem for Panpsychism" (2016) establishes that the Combination Problem is not a single puzzle, but a complex cluster of interrelated metaphysical hurdles:
1. **The Subject Combination Problem**: William James's original dilemma. Subjects cannot simply sum into a single subject. If subject $A$ experiences joy, and subject $B$ experiences sorrow, their aggregation is a world containing two subjects; it does not yield a third unified subject $C$ experiencing mixed emotions.
2. **The Quality Combination Problem**: Even if subjects combined, how do simple, basic micro-qualities (perhaps electrical charge-feeling or spin-feeling) generate the specific qualitative character of phenomenal red, musical pitch, or olfactory sensations? This mirrors the classic "palette problem" and the "inconceivability of quality emergence".
3. **The Structural Mismatch Problem**: Phenomenal consciousness exhibits a specific, continuous, holistic topological geometry (e.g., the unified 3D visual field), whereas the micro-physical brain is a discontinuous, granular, discrete aggregate of trillions of separate cells and subatomic particles.

Because constitutive micro-panpsychism struggles so severely with Subject Combination, contemporary philosophers such as Philip Goff (*Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 2017) and Itay Shani have turned to **Cosmopsychism**: the view that the *universe as a whole* is the single fundamental conscious subject, and human minds are derived by "de-combination" or individuation from the cosmic subject, thereby bypassing James’s bottom-up combination dilemma entirely.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Quantum decoherence destroys quantum coherence; it does not explain how distinct subjective viewpoints merge into one unified phenomenal perspective.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Chalmers, Goff, and Seager demonstrate that Quality Combination and Structural Mismatch are profound conceptual, metaphysical problems that mathematical Fourier transforms cannot touch (qualia are not mere mathematical wave decompositions).
- **(D) is incorrect**: The Subject Combination problem is the defining internal crisis of *panpsychism*, not Cartesian dualism (which posits an indivisible, single soul from the outset).

**Primary Textual Reference**: David J. Chalmers, "The Combination Problem for Panpsychism", in *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives*, ed. G. Bruntrup and L. Jaskolla (Oxford University Press, 2016), pp. 179–214; William James, *The Principles of Psychology* (Henry Holt, 1890), Chapter 6: "The Mind-Stuff Theory", pp. 158–162.

### Question 3: Higher-Order Thought (HOT) Theory and the Problem of Targetless HOTs
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: David Rosenthal (1986, 2005), Peter Carruthers (2000), Ned Block (2011)

In David Rosenthal’s **Higher-Order Thought (HOT) Theory of Consciousness**, a mental state $M$ (e.g., a visual perception of red) is conscious if and only if there is a non-inferential, assertoric higher-order thought $HOT$ asserting: *"I am currently in mental state $M$."*

Ned Block and other critics confront Rosenthal with the dilemma of **Targetless HOTs** (or misrepresentative HOTs): Suppose a subject’s brain, through pathological wiring or artificial TMS stimulation, generates a higher-order thought asserting: *"I am currently in pain"*, but there is *no lower-order first-order state of pain whatsoever* occurring in the sensory cortex. What lethal dilemma does this pose for Rosenthal’s theory?

- (A) It shows that the person becomes a philosophical zombie whose motor cortex stops functioning permanently.
- (B) It proves that all sensory pain is an optical illusion located entirely in the occipital lobe.
- (C) It demonstrates that higher-order thoughts violate the principle of non-contradiction in classical modal logic.
- (D) If Rosenthal claims the person feels pain, then consciousness does not require first-order sensory states at all (a purely intellectual thought generates phenomenal qualia out of nothing); if he claims the person does not feel pain, then higher-order thoughts are insufficient for consciousness, refuting the foundational biconditional of HOT theory.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
David Rosenthal’s HOT theory defines state consciousness reductively:
$$\text{Mental state } M \text{ is conscious } \iff \exists \text{ } HOT \text{ such that } HOT \text{ represents that } I \text{ am in } M.$$
The core vulnerability of this theory is the **Separability Thesis**: because the first-order state $M$ and the higher-order thought $HOT$ are two distinct, independent mental states (governed by Hume's dictum that distinct existences can exist separately), it is conceptually and physically possible for them to come apart.

This generates two catastrophic possibilities:
1. *Misrepresentative HOT*: The first-order state is a perception of green, but the HOT represents: *"I am seeing red."* What does the subject consciously experience? Red or green?
2. *Targetless HOT*: There is *no* first-order state at all, but the brain generates a HOT: *"I am experiencing sharp pain in my left wrist."*

This presents a fatal dilemma for Rosenthal:
- *Horn 1*: If Rosenthal claims the subject *does* consciously feel pain, then a higher-order cognitive, conceptual thought (which is devoid of sensory qualia) magically generates phenomenal sensory qualia out of thin air without any sensory base. This abandons the thesis that consciousness is a property of *sensory states*.
- *Horn 2*: If Rosenthal claims the subject *does not* feel pain (because there is no first-order pain to be conscious of), then having a HOT is *not sufficient* for phenomenal consciousness, which directly refutes the foundational biconditional claim of HOT theory.

Rosenthal attempts to bite Horn 1 by arguing that the subject experiences "intentional qualia" or that phenomenal character is purely intentional, but critics (Block, Byrne, Neander) demonstrate that this reduces phenomenal experience to mere ungrounded thought.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: The subject does not become a physical zombie; the person's cognitive system continues running, but the theory cannot coherently account for what it feels like from the inside.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Pain is processed in the somatosensory cortex, insular cortex, and anterior cingulate, not the occipital lobe, and Targetless HOT is a philosophical argument, not an optical illusion.
- **(C) is incorrect**: There is no logical contradiction ($P \land \neg P$); the problem is an empirical and metaphysical dilemma about what constitutes phenomenal experience.

**Primary Textual Reference**: David M. Rosenthal, *Consciousness and Mind* (Oxford University Press, 2005), Chapter 7: "Sensory Quality and the Contents of Higher-Order Thoughts"; Ned Block, "The Higher Order Approach to Consciousness is Defunct", *Analysis* 71(3) (2011): 419–431.

### Question 4: David Chalmers's Taxonomy of Materialism: Type-A, Type-B, and Type-C
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: David J. Chalmers ("Consciousness and its Place in Nature", 2002)

In his definitive survey "Consciousness and its Place in Nature" (2002), David Chalmers mapped all possible responses to the mind-body problem based on how they treat the **Epistemic Gap** (Jackson's Mary, Chalmers's Zombies, Levine's Explanatory Gap) and whether that epistemic gap entails an **Ontological Gap** (Dualism). How does Chalmers formally distinguish **Type-A Materialism**, **Type-B Materialism**, and **Type-C Materialism**?

- (A) **Type-A Materialism** denies that there is an epistemic gap (holding that Mary learns no new facts and zombies are strictly inconceivable; e.g., Dennett, Armstrong); **Type-B Materialism** accepts an epistemic gap but denies that it entails an ontological gap (holding that zombies are conceivable but metaphysically impossible due to strong *a posteriori* psychophysical identities; e.g., Block, Loar, Hill); and **Type-C Materialism** holds that the epistemic gap currently exists for our limited human minds but would close upon future scientific enlightenment, meaning zombies are temporarily conceivable but will eventually become inconceivable.
- (B) Type-A believes in angels, Type-B believes in biological blood, and Type-C believes in computer software.
- (C) Type-A claims that physical matter is made of atoms, Type-B claims it is made of water, and Type-C claims it is made of fire.
- (D) All three types of materialism agree that Cartesian substance dualism is the only correct scientific view of reality.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Chalmers's classification provides the standard architecture for the contemporary philosophy of mind:
```
                               Does an Epistemic Gap Exist?
                                     /             \
                                   NO               YES
                                  /                   \
                         [Type-A Materialism]   Does Epistemic Gap Entail
                           (Denies gap;           an Ontological Gap?
                            Eliminativism /            /        \
                            Analytic Functionalism)  NO          YES
                                                    /              \
                                          [Type-B Materialism]   [Dualism / Type-D & E]
                                            (Accepts gap, denies   (Property Dualism,
                                             ontological gap;       Substance Dualism,
                                             A Posteriori Identity) Epiphenomenalism)
```
1. **Type-A Materialism (Deflationary Physicalism / Eliminativism)**:
   - Dennett, Paul Churchland, David Lewis, David Armstrong.
   - There is no epistemic gap between physical facts and phenomenal facts.
   - When Mary is in her black-and-white room, she already knows everything, and upon leaving, she gains no factual propositional knowledge. A philosophical zombie is not even coherent or conceivable; if a system has all physical and functional processes, consciousness is conceptually entailed *a priori*.
2. **Type-B Materialism (A Posteriori Physicalism / The Phenomenal Concept Strategy)**:
   - Ned Block, Christopher Hill, Brian Loar, Janet Levin.
   - Concedes to Chalmers and Jackson that there is a genuine, unbridgeable *a priori* epistemic gap: you cannot deduce qualia from neurobiology, and zombies are conceptually conceivable.
   - **The Core Move**: Denies that conceivability entails metaphysical possibility!
   - Just as it was conceivable to ancient Babylonians that Hesperus was not Phosphorus (even though they are identical in all possible worlds), it is conceivable that brain state $P$ exists without phenomenal state $Q$, yet they are identical *a posteriori* ($P = Q$).
3. **Type-C Materialism (Epistemic / Temporary Physicalism)**:
   - Thomas Nagel (early), Colin McGinn.
   - The epistemic gap appears unbridgeable today only because of our primitive scientific concepts. If we undergo a conceptual revolution (analogous to the shift from pre-Newtonian physics to quantum mechanics), the gap will close, and we will realize that zombies were inconceivable all along.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B) is incorrect**: Uses colloquial theological and biological puns that have nothing to do with analytic modal epistemology.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Conflates modal materialism with Pre-Socratic cosmology (Thales, Heraclitus).
- **(D) is incorrect**: All three types of materialism are *materialist* doctrines; they explicitly reject Cartesian substance dualism.

**Primary Textual Reference**: David J. Chalmers, "Consciousness and its Place in Nature", in *Philosophy of Mind: Classical and Contemporary Readings*, ed. D. J. Chalmers (Oxford University Press, 2002), pp. 247–272; reprinted in *The Character of Consciousness* (Oxford University Press, 2010), Chapter 3.

### Question 5: Saul Kripke and the Modal Argument Against Type Identity
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Saul Kripke (*Naming and Necessity*, 1972/1980)

In Lecture III of *Naming and Necessity* (1980), Saul Kripke mounted a devastating modal argument that shattered the classical Type Identity Theory of Smart, Place, and Armstrong. Identity theorists had defended $\text{Pain} = \text{C-fiber firing}$ by comparing it to scientific theoretical identities like $\text{Water} = \text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{Heat} = \text{Molecular kinetic energy}$, arguing that while the identity is known *a posteriori*, it could have been otherwise. What is Kripke's profound modal refutation of this physicalist analogy?

- (A) Theoretical identities like $\text{Water} = \text{H}_2\text{O}$ connect two **rigid designators** and are therefore **metaphysically necessary in all possible worlds** ($\Box(A = B)$); the apparent contingency of $\text{Heat} = \text{Motion}$ is an epistemic illusion explained away by the contingent relation between heat and its *contingent intermediary sensation*; however, because pain is picked out by its **essential, intrinsic phenomenal property** (something is a pain if and only if it *feels* like a pain, with no intermediary between pain and its appearance), the apparent contingency of $\text{Pain} = \text{C-fiber firing}$ cannot be explained away as an epistemic illusion, meaning the mind-brain identity is either necessary or strictly false.
- (B) The argument claims that C-fibers only exist in dead cadavers.
- (C) Kripke demonstrates that names in English have zero meaning in modal logic.
- (D) Kripke proved that water is made of helium and oxygen in counterfactual multiverses.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Kripke revolutionized modal metaphysics and philosophy of mind:
1. **The Necessity of Identity**:
   - If $A$ and $B$ are **rigid designators** (terms that pick out the exact same object in every possible world where it exists), then if $A = B$ is true, it is **necessarily true**:
     $$\forall x \forall y (x = y \to \Box(x = y))$$
   - "Water" and "$\text{H}_2\text{O}$" are rigid designators. Therefore, if water is $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, it is *necessarily* $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ in all possible worlds.
2. **The Illusion of Contingency in Science**:
   - Why did people think "Heat is the motion of molecules" was contingent?
   - Kripke explains: We pick out heat in the actual world via an accidental, contingent property: *the sensation it produces in human nerve endings* (its reference-fixing description).
   - We can easily imagine a possible world where molecular kinetic energy does not produce the sensation of heat (e.g., if human skin had different sensory receptors), or a world where something else (like cosmic rays) produces that sensation.
   - We confuse this with the false thought that *heat itself* could exist without molecular motion.
3. **The Fatal Breakdown in the Case of Pain**:
   - "Pain" and "C-fiber firing" are both rigid designators. Therefore, if $\text{Pain} = \text{C-fiber firing}$, it must be **necessarily true** ($\Box(\text{Pain} = \text{C-fiber firing})$).
   - But we can clearly conceive of:
     1. A possible world where there is **pain without C-fiber firing** (an alien or ghost feeling pain);
     2. A possible world where there is **C-fiber firing without pain** (a philosophical zombie).
   - Can the physicalist explain this away using the "sensation intermediary" trick? **NO!**
   - For pain, **the appearance IS the reality**. Pain is not picked out by a contingent sensation it causes in something else; *pain is the sensation itself*! There is no gap between being in pain and feeling pain.
   - Because the appearance of pain cannot be separated from pain, the conceivability of pain without C-fiber firing cannot be explained away as an epistemic illusion. Therefore, pain can exist without C-fiber firing, and they are **not identical**.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B) is incorrect**: C-fibers are real unmyelinated peripheral and central axons in living biological organisms.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Kripke's direct reference theory proves that proper names and natural kind terms have direct referents across modal possible worlds, not zero meaning.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Water is rigidly $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ across all metaphysically possible worlds; Kripke proves that water could *never* be helium in any possible world.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Saul A. Kripke, *Naming and Necessity* (Harvard University Press, 1980), Lecture III, pp. 144–155; originally published in *Semantics of Natural Language*, ed. D. Davidson and G. Harman (D. Reidel, 1972).

### Question 6: Keith Frankish and Daniel Dennett on Illusionism about Qualia
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Keith Frankish ("Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness", 2016), Daniel C. Dennett (*Consciousness Explained*, 1991; 2016)

In his influential 2016 target article in the *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, Keith Frankish coined the term **"Illusionism"** to describe the radical physicalist program championed by Daniel Dennett and Derk Pereboom. Rather than trying to solve the "Hard Problem" (explaining how physical matter generates phenomenal qualia), what does Illusionism assert regarding phenomenal consciousness?

- (A) Frankish argues that human brains are physical illusions that vanish when the lights are turned off.
- (B) Illusionism claims that computers feel intense pain whenever an error code occurs in software compilation.
- (C) Illusionism is an optical magic show performed on stage using mirrors and stage lights.
- (D) Phenomenal consciousness (defined as private, ineffable, non-physical qualia) simply does not exist; it is an introspective illusion generated by complex cognitive tracking mechanisms, meaning that the true scientific task is not the "Hard Problem", but the "Illusion Problem": explaining why the brain's introspective user-interface leads us to falsely believe we possess magical qualitative states.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Keith Frankish divided the philosophy of consciousness into two irreconcilable camps:
1. **Realism about Phenomenal Consciousness**:
   - Accepts that phenomenal consciousness (*qualia*) really exists.
   - This leads directly into the Hard Problem (Chalmers), Dualism, or Panpsychism, because phenomenal qualia cannot be reduced to physical-functional processes.
2. **Illusionism (Frankish, Dennett, Pereboom)**:
   - Rejects the existence of phenomenal qualia altogether:
     > *"Illusionism replaces the Hard Problem with the Illusion Problem: the problem of explaining why we mistakenly think we have phenomenal consciousness."*
3. **The Core Illusionist Mechanics**:
   - The brain possesses introspective mechanisms that monitor its own internal cognitive processes.
   - However, just like a computer's graphical desktop interface represents complex binary voltages as friendly "folders" and "trash cans", the brain's introspective monitor represents complex non-phenomenal sensory classifications as simplified, non-physical, qualitative "feels" (**quasi-phenomenal properties**).
   - Humans fall victim to an introspective cognitive error: we mistake the simplified representational user-interface for an actual, non-physical property residing in our souls.
   - Therefore, there are no qualia to be explained by physics; science only needs to explain the evolutionary and cognitive etiology of the introspective illusion.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Illusionism is an uncompromising materialist position; it affirms that the physical brain, neurons, and functional states are 100% real.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Illusionism denies that even humans have phenomenal pain; it would never attribute phenomenal pain to software compilation errors.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Stage illusionism is theatrical entertainment; philosophical illusionism is a rigorous ontological thesis about introspective representation in cognitive science.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Keith Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23, no. 11–12 (2016): 11–39; Daniel C. Dennett, *Consciousness Explained* (Little, Brown and Co., 1991); Derk Pereboom, *Consciousness and the Prospects of Physicalism* (Oxford University Press, 2011).

### Question 7: David Chalmers and the "Meta-Problem" of Consciousness
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: David J. Chalmers ("The Meta-Problem of Consciousness", 2018)

In 2018, David Chalmers published a landmark paper introducing **"The Meta-Problem of Consciousness"**. While the **Hard Problem** asks *why and how physical processes give rise to conscious experience*, what is the **Meta-Problem**, and why does Chalmers argue it is strictly an **"Easy Problem"** that physicalists and dualists alike must answer?

- (A) The Meta-Problem is the purely empirical, functional problem of explaining why human beings *report* that they have a hard problem—explaining the cognitive, psychological, and evolutionary mechanisms that cause humans to assert that consciousness is puzzling, to write philosophy papers about qualia, and to intuit that zombies are possible; it is an "easy problem" because it concerns explaining physical behavior and verbal utterances.
- (B) The Meta-Problem is the technical challenge of optimizing the Meta Quest virtual reality headset refresh rate.
- (C) The Meta-Problem asks whether God has a conscious mind in higher-dimensional string theory.
- (D) Chalmers argues that the Meta-Problem proves that consciousness was solved by Isaac Newton in 1687.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
David Chalmers’s formulation of the Meta-Problem transformed the contemporary dialectic:
1. **The Hard Problem vs. The Meta-Problem**:
   - **The Hard Problem**: Why should physical brain processing feel like anything from the inside? (A metaphysical problem about the ontological status of subjective experience).
   - **The Meta-Problem**: Why do humans *say* they have a hard problem? Why do we have intuitions that qualia are non-physical? Why do people write papers about Mary the color scientist or debate philosophical zombies?
2. **Why the Meta-Problem is Tractable (An "Easy Problem")**:
   - Verbal utterances, button presses, behavioral reports, and book publications are **physical, observable events in spacetime**.
   - Explaining why a human opens their mouth and says *"I am experiencing a deep mystery about red"* requires only cognitive science, evolutionary psychology, linguistic semantics, and computational neuroscience. It does not require solving the ontological Hard Problem.
3. **The Strategic Dialectic for Theories of Consciousness**:
   - Any adequate theory of consciousness must solve the Meta-Problem:
     - **For the Illusionist (Dennett, Frankish)**: A solution to the meta-problem *dissolves* the hard problem. If you explain why the brain's cognitive architecture generates the illusion and verbal reports of qualia, there is nothing left to explain!
     - **For the Realist / Dualist (Chalmers)**: Even if you explain why we talk about qualia, you still haven't explained the actual qualitative experience itself. However, the realist must still show how the cognitive mechanism that produces meta-problem reports is reliably connected to the real conscious qualia it reports on (avoiding epiphenomenalist skepticism).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B) is incorrect**: Conflates the Greek prefix *meta-* with the commercial brand name of Meta Platforms / VR headsets.
- **(C) is incorrect**: The Meta-Problem is a naturalistic cognitive science problem about human psychological reports, not theological speculation about higher-dimensional gods.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Newton never addressed the Hard Problem or the Meta-Problem of consciousness; Newton was concerned with optics and gravitation.

**Primary Textual Reference**: David J. Chalmers, "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 25, no. 9–10 (2018): 6–61; see also the open-peer commentaries in the same volume.

### Question 8: Russellian Monism and the Inscrutability of Matter: Structural Realism and Intrinsic Nature
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Bertrand Russell (*The Analysis of Matter*, 1927), Arthur Eddington (1928), Michael Lockwood (1989), David Chalmers (1996, 2013)

**Russellian Monism** (reviving Bertrand Russell's thesis in *The Analysis of Matter*, 1927, and Arthur Eddington's 1928 Gifford Lectures) is widely regarded as one of the most promising alternatives to both standard physicalism and Cartesian dualism. What is Russell's foundational insight regarding the **"Inscrutability of Matter"**, and how does it solve the mind-body problem?

- (A) Matter is inscrutable because physicists are forbidden by law from publishing equations about subatomic particles.
- (B) Russell argued that all matter was destroyed during the First World War.
- (C) Physics reveals only the abstract, mathematical, and relational/dispositional structure of matter (e.g., mass is resistance to acceleration; charge is mutual attraction/repulsion), leaving the **intrinsic, categorical nature** of what physical entities are in themselves completely unknown and inscrutable; Russellian Monism posits that the intrinsic categorical natures of fundamental physical entities are proto-phenomenal or phenomenal properties, seamlessly unifying the physical relational structure of the world with conscious interiority.
- (D) Russellian Monism claims that consciousness exists only when an electron rotates clockwise in a magnetic field.

**Correct Answer**: **(C)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Russellian Monism identifies a blind spot in 400 years of physics:
1. **The Structural Limitation of Physical Science**:
   - When physics defines an electron, what does it tell us?
     - Mass: $9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$ (a disposition to resist acceleration under force: $F = ma$).
     - Charge: $-1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$ (a disposition to repel like charges and attract opposite charges).
     - Spin: $1/2$.
   - **Eddington's Insight**: Every physical quantity is defined in terms of other physical relations. Physics gives us a schedule of equations showing how things relate and affect one another: pure mathematical **relational and dispositional structure**.
   - But what is the entity *in itself* that has these dispositions? Dispositions require a **categorical base**: relations must be relations between *things that have an intrinsic nature*!
   - As Russell famously wrote in *The Analysis of Matter* (1927):
     > *"Physics is mathematical not because we know so much about the physical world, but because we know so little: it is only its mathematical properties that we can discover. For the rest, our knowledge is negative... As regards the world in itself, we know nothing."*
2. **The Intrinsic Nature of Matter**:
   - There is only **one place in the entire universe** where we have direct, non-relational access to the intrinsic nature of a physical system: **our own conscious experience**!
   - In conscious experience, we know phenomenal redness, pain, and joy not as abstract equations, but as they are in themselves (their categorical nature).
3. **The Russellian Monist Synthesis**:
   - What if the intrinsic nature of matter—the hidden categorical ground of the equations of physics—is **phenomenal or proto-phenomenal**?
   - From the outside (physics), the brain looks like an electro-chemical relational network.
   - From the inside (consciousness), we experience its intrinsic nature directly.
   - This avoids dualism (there is only one substance, physical reality) and avoids reductive physicalism (consciousness is not denied or reduced to mere behavior; it is the intrinsic essence of the physical world).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Scientific secrecy or legal restrictions have nothing to do with the epistemological and metaphysical limit of structural mathematical physics.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Conflates Russell's political pacifism during WWI with his analytical philosophy of physics and mind.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Russellian Monism is a general metaphysical thesis about the intrinsic categorical grounds of all physical structure, not a specific claim about clockwise electron spin.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Bertrand Russell, *The Analysis of Matter* (Kegan Paul, 1927), Chapter 36 ("The Interpretation of Physics"); Arthur Eddington, *The Nature of the Physical World* (Cambridge University Press, 1928); David J. Chalmers, "Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism", in *The Conscious Mind in Action*, ed. G. Bruntrup and L. Jaskolla (Oxford University Press, 2013).

### Question 9: Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0) and the Unfolding Argument
**Format**: Formal Dialectical Argument (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Giulio Tononi, Larissa Albantakis (*IIT 4.0*, 2023); Michael Herzog, Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger ("The Unfolding Argument", 2019)

**Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** posits that consciousness is an intrinsic, fundamental property of physical systems, quantitatively measured by integrated information ($\Phi^{	ext{max}}$), which requires non-zero feedback and causal recurrence. In 2019, Doerig, Schurger, Hessinger, and Herzog published **"The Unfolding Argument"** challenging IIT's scientific testability. What is the core mathematical and philosophical thrust of the Unfolding Argument?

- (A) The unfolding argument proves that feedforward networks violate the laws of thermodynamics when calculating matrix multiplications.
- (B) The unfolding argument shows that consciousness can only emerge in biological carbon compounds, ruling out silicon computers a priori.
- (C) IIT 4.0 proves that the human cerebellum has a higher $\Phi$ than the cerebral cortex, demonstrating that motor coordination is the supreme form of consciousness.
- (D) Any recurrent neural network (which IIT claims has high $\Phi$ and is conscious) can be mathematically unfolded into an input-output equivalent purely feedforward neural network (which IIT dictates has $\Phi = 0$ and is completely non-conscious); therefore, unless IIT assumes consciousness is independent of all input-output behavior, IIT's claims about consciousness are either unfalsifiable or dissociate consciousness completely from empirical cognitive function.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
The debate between IIT (Tononi et al.) and the Unfolding Argument (Doerig et al., *PNAS* 2019) is one of the most fierce contemporary disputes in neurophilosophy:
1. **IIT's Postulate**:
   - IIT 4.0 asserts that consciousness is identical to the maximal intrinsic cause-effect structure of a system ($\Phi^{	ext{max}}$).
   - Recurrent architectures with complex feedback loops generate high $\Phi$. Feedforward networks (which have no causal loops) have $\Phi = 0$, meaning they possess zero phenomenal experience, regardless of how intelligently they act.
2. **The Unfolding Theorem**:
   - According to classical automata theory and the Krohn-Rhodes theorem, for any discrete-time recurrent neural network (RNN) operating over a finite time horizon, there exists a feedforward network (FFN) that reproduces the exact same input-output mapping.
   - Doerig et al. argue that if an experimenter tests a system via psychophysical reports, tasks, or behavior, the RNN and unfolded FFN will behave identically.
   - If the theorist claims the RNN is conscious while the unfolded FFN is a non-conscious zombie, this attribution can never be verified by any behavioral, functional, or cognitive test.
3. **The Dilemma for IIT**:
   - IIT must either accept that consciousness has no necessary connection to input-output function (embracing a radical form of non-functionalist panpsychism/isolationism) or admit that $\Phi$ is not empirically testable via standard scientific methodology.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: The unfolding theorem is an established theorem of computational automata theory, having nothing to do with thermodynamic violations.
- **(B) is incorrect**: IIT is substrate-independent; it allows consciousness in silicon provided it has recurrent intrinsic cause-effect integration ($\Phi > 0$).
- **(C) is incorrect**: IIT famously notes that the cerebellum has 4 times more neurons than the cortex but lacks consciousness precisely because its architecture is largely feedforward with low $\Phi$; this is an IIT claim, not the unfolding argument.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, Kathryn Hessinger, and Michael H. Herzog, "The Unfolding Argument: Why IIT and Other Causal Structure Theories Cannot Explain Consciousness", *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 116, no. 49 (2019): 24397–24404; Larissa Albantakis et al., "Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the Properties of Phenomenal Existence in Physical Terms", *PLOS Computational Biology* 19, no. 10 (2023): e1011465.

### Question 10: Global Neuronal Workspace vs. Posterior Hot Zone: The Adversarial Collaboration
**Format**: Empirical-Dialectical Evaluation (Format B)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Stanislas Dehaene (GNWT), Christof Koch (IIT), Cogitate Consortium (*Nature / Science*, 2023)

In 2023, the **Cogitate Consortium** released the first results of an internationally preregistered **Adversarial Collaboration** directly pitting **Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT)** against **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** to localize the neural substrates of visual consciousness. What was the central point of anatomical and temporal divergence between the two theories, and how did the empirical data challenge GNWT?

- (A) The experiment proved that visual qualia do not exist, and that both theories are completely false.
- (B) GNWT predicted that consciousness is localized in the spinal cord, while IIT predicted it resides in the olfactory bulb.
- (C) GNWT predicts that consciousness requires late (300–400 ms) ignition and sustained activity in the **prefrontal cortex (PFC)** with frontoparietal broadcasting, whereas IIT predicts that consciousness is generated continuously in a **"posterior hot zone"** (parieto-occipital-temporal sensory areas); the empirical findings failed to detect sustained prefrontal representation of stimulus content during continuous conscious perception, delivering a major challenge to classic GNWT.
- (D) The empirical data showed that prefrontal ignition occurs at 10 milliseconds, far faster than synaptic transmission allows.

**Correct Answer**: **(C)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
The Cogitate Consortium (Melloni et al., *Nature* 2023) represents a milestone in the philosophy and cognitive neuroscience of consciousness:
1. **The Opposing Hypotheses**:
   - **GNWT (Dehaene, Changeux)**: Visual experience occurs when perceptual representations are "broadcast" globally via long-range axons to the prefrontal cortex (PFC). Critical predictions: (1) An ignition marker ($P3b$ wave at $\sim 300	ext{–}400$ ms); (2) Sustained prefrontal decoding of the conscious content throughout the duration of the percept; (3) Frontoparietal synchronization.
   - **IIT (Koch, Tononi)**: Consciousness is determined by maximum cause-effect power, which anatomical grid structures in the **posterior cortical hot zone** (temporo-parieto-occipital areas) possess, while the prefrontal cortex mediates task-monitoring, motor planning, and verbal reporting rather than the experience itself.
2. **The 2023 Empirical Outcome**:
   - Using multimodal fMRI, MEG, and intracranial electrophysiology (iEEG) across multiple independent laboratories:
   - The posterior hot zone showed sustained, robust encoding of visual contents across time, supporting IIT.
   - In contrast, the prefrontal cortex showed transient onset and offset responses, but **failed to maintain sustained content decoding** during continuous perception, challenging the core GNWT claim that sustained prefrontal broadcasting constitutes the conscious state.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: The experiment was designed to test rival neural theories of consciousness, not to claim qualia do not exist.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Neither theory locates consciousness in the spinal cord or olfactory bulb; both focus on neocortical networks.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Synaptic transmission cannot occur at 10 ms for cortical broadcasting; GNWT’s marker is the $P3b$ wave occurring around 300–400 ms.

**Primary Textual Reference**: The Cogitate Consortium et al., "An Adversarial Collaboration to Critically Evaluate Theories of Consciousness", *bioRxiv / Nature* (2023); Stanislas Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts* (Viking, 2014); Christof Koch, *The Feeling of Life Itself: Why Consciousness Is Widespread but Can't Be Computed* (MIT Press, 2019).

### Question 11: The Combination Problem for Panpsychism and Cosmopsychic Monism
**Format**: Metaphysical Dialectic (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: William James (*The Principles of Psychology*, 1890); David J. Chalmers ("The Combination Problem for Panpsychism", 2016); Philip Goff (*Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 2017)

The most formidable objection to constitutive micro-panpsychism is **The Combination Problem**, first posed by William James (1890) and formalized by David Chalmers (2016). Chalmers decomposes it into three distinct sub-problems:
1. The **Subject Combination Problem**: How do billions of distinct micro-subjects combine to form a single unified macro-subject ($10^{11}$ micro-minds $	o$ 1 human mind)?
2. The **Quality Combination Problem**: How do micro-qualities (e.g., proto-charge qualia) combine to yield macro-qualities (e.g., the taste of wine)?
3. The **Structural Combination Problem**: How does the micro-structure combine into macro-phenomenal structure?

How does **Cosmopsychism** (defended by Philip Goff and Yujin Nagasawa) solve the Subject Combination Problem by inverting the direction of ontological priority?

- (A) It claims that micro-subjects combine mechanically through covalent chemical bonding in the cerebral ventricles.
- (B) Instead of building a macro-conscious subject bottom-up out of trillions of micro-subjects, Cosmopsychism asserts that the entire universe (*the Cosmos*) is the single, fundamental, primary subject of consciousness, and individual human minds derive their consciousness via **top-down differentiation / de-combination** (analogous to how multiple alters dissociate within a single mind in dissociative identity disorder), completely avoiding the bottom-up combination problem.
- (C) Cosmopsychism solves the problem by denying that human beings have conscious experiences, claiming that only stars and black holes are conscious.
- (D) It asserts that consciousness is an emergent illusion produced by digital computing algorithms running on alien supercomputers.

**Correct Answer**: **(B)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
The Combination Problem is widely regarded as the "Hard Problem" for panpsychism:
1. **The Classic Jamesian Objection (1890)**:
   - William James wrote: *"Take a hundred of them, shuffle them and pack them as close together as you can (whatever that may mean); still each remains the same it was... Where the hundredth ceases, the ninety-ninth begins. There is no aggregate there, in the hundred, which was not in each of the individuals."*
   - Two people sitting next to each other do not combine into a third, overarching supra-person. Why should billions of micro-experiential particles combine into a single human conscious subject?
2. **The Cosmopsychist Solution (Top-Down Monism)**:
   - Drawing on Spinozistic and Schafferian Priority Monism, **Cosmopsychism** inverts the fundamental mereology.
   - The fundamental ontological entity is not the micro-particle, but the whole universe: **The Cosmos**.
   - The cosmos as a whole is a conscious subject. Human beings and other conscious creatures are not bottom-up aggregates of atomic minds; rather, they are localized, semi-dissociated aspects or modes of the cosmic subject (**The De-Combination / Differentiation Problem**).
   - Resolving how a single unified mind dissociates into localized perspectives (a phenomenon with empirical precedent in clinical dissociation and split-brain phenomena) is widely considered far more metaphysically tractable than explaining how distinct subjects fuse into one.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Chemical bonding describes physical valence interactions between atoms; it does nothing to explain the metaphysical fusion of subjective points of view.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Cosmopsychists do not deny human consciousness; their goal is precisely to provide a metaphysical ground for human experience.
- **(D) is incorrect**: This is the Simulation Hypothesis (Nick Bostrom), not metaphysical cosmopsychism.

**Primary Textual Reference**: David J. Chalmers, "The Combination Problem for Panpsychism", in *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives*, ed. Godehard Brüntrup and Ludwig Jaskolla (Oxford University Press, 2016), pp. 179–214; Philip Goff, *Consciousness and Fundamental Reality* (Oxford University Press, 2017); Yujin Nagasawa, *Maximal God: A New Defence of Perfect Being Theism* (Oxford University Press, 2017).

### Question 12: Radical Enactivism and the "Hard Problem of Content"
**Format**: Conceptual / Dialectical (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Daniel D. Hutto and Erik Myin (*Radicalizing Enactivism*, 2013; *Evolving Enactivism*, 2017)

In *Radicalizing Enactivism* (2013), Daniel Hutto and Erik Myin formulate **The Hard Problem of Content** to challenge both traditional cognitivism and moderate forms of enactivism (e.g., Alva Noë, Francisco Varela). What is the Hard Problem of Content, and how does Radical Enactivism (REC) address it?

- (A) The mathematical proof that neural networks cannot perform backpropagation without symbolic programming languages.
- (B) The claim that all biological cells possess innate understanding of Shakespearean English.
- (C) Informational covariation (natural information, such as tree rings indicating age or smoke indicating fire) does not possess truth-conditions, reference, or semantic accuracy; teleosemantics and biological functionalism fail to naturalize semantic content because biological selection operates on successful action, not truth. Therefore, basic cognition is completely non-representational and non-contentful (**Ur-Intentionality**), with contentful representations emerging only later through socially scaffolded, socio-cultural linguistic practices.
- (D) The problem of how to compress multi-gigabyte video files into neural synapses without loss of visual resolution.

**Correct Answer**: **(C)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Daniel Hutto and Erik Myin's Radical Enactive Cognition (REC) presents a major challenge to representational cognitive science:
1. **The Failure of Naturalized Teleosemantics**:
   - For decades, physicalists (Ruth Millikan, Fred Dretske, Jerry Fodor) attempted to naturalize representational content via teleosemantics (selection history) and informational tracking.
   - Hutto and Myin argue that this fails to generate **semantic content**. A thermostat or a tree ring covaries with temperature, but it does not represent the world *as* being a certain way—it has no truth-conditions, cannot make claims, and cannot be mistaken in a semantic sense.
2. **The Hard Problem of Content**:
   - How can purely physical, covariance-based biological systems ever generate genuine representational content with truth-evaluable conditions?
   - REC's radical answer: **They don't.** Basic biological perception and action are entirely **non-representational**.
   - An animal running from a predator is not evaluating propositions or consulting internal mental maps; it is engaged in direct, extensive, embodied, sensorimotor engagement with its environment (**Ur-Intentionality** or intentionality without content).
3. **Where Content Comes From**:
   - Genuine representational content arrives only with the evolutionary advent of public, socio-cultural, language-involving conventions and institutional practices.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Irrelevant technical distracter; REC is a philosophical critique of the representational foundation of cognitive science.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Absurd caricature; REC denies that even basic human perceptions have innate semantic content, let alone biological cells.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Conflates philosophical semantic content (meaning, truth-conditions) with computer file compression.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Daniel D. Hutto and Erik Myin, *Radicalizing Enactivism: Basic Minds without Content* (MIT Press, 2013); Daniel D. Hutto and Erik Myin, *Evolving Enactivism: Basic Minds Meet Content* (MIT Press, 2017).

### Question 13: The Phenomenal Intentionality Research Program (PIRP)
**Format**: Conceptual / Dialectical (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Terry Horgan, John Tienson, George Graham ("Phenomenal Intentionality and the Brain in a Vat", 2004); Galen Strawson (*Mental Reality*, 1994)

The **Phenomenal Intentionality Research Program (PIRP)** directly challenges the prevailing externalist consensus in philosophy of mind (Putnam, Burge, Dretske) by defending **Internalism about Original Intentionality**. Consider Terry Horgan and John Tienson's thought experiment of the **Phenomenal Duplicate Brain-in-a-Vat (BIV)**: a brain maintained in a vat whose phenomenal experiences are introspectively and qualitatively indistinguishable from your own. What does PIRP deduce about the intentionality of this BIV?

- (A) The BIV proves that consciousness is impossible without a biological spinal cord and digestive tract.
- (B) The BIV possesses genuine, rich, original intentionality directed at its phenomenally constituted world; original mental aboutness is fundamentally grounded in and constituted by phenomenal consciousness (*phenomenal intentionality*), proving that phenomenal consciousness is ontologically prior to and independent of external causal and tracking relations.
- (C) The BIV possesses absolutely zero intentional states, thoughts, or beliefs because it is not physically situated in an external social community.
- (D) The BIV's thoughts are identical to the electrical oscillations of the life-support machine, showing that computers have identical minds to humans.

**Correct Answer**: **(B)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
The Phenomenal Intentionality Research Program (PIRP) is one of the most significant 21st-century revivals of internalist intentionality (Horgan & Tienson 2002; Kriegel 2011; Bourget & Mendelovici 2014):
1. **The Externalist Dogma Challenged**:
   - Externalists (Dretske, Fodor, Millikan) argue that intentionality is constituted by causal tracking relations between the brain and external objects. Under strict externalism, a newly created BIV or Swampman has no tracking history and therefore has no beliefs, thoughts, or representational content whatsoever.
2. **PIRP's Counter-Intuition**:
   - Imagine a duplicate brain in a vat that has phenomenal experiences identical to yours: it feels the warmth of the sun, sees a red apple, deliberates about a mathematical proof, and yearns for friendship.
   - To say that this creature has *no thoughts*, *no intentional directedness*, and *no mental aboutness* is phenomenologically and conceptually unconvincing.
   - It possesses rich **phenomenal intentionality**: the aboutness that is intrinsic to the subjective character of the phenomenal experience itself.
3. **The Core Thesis of PIRP**:
   - **Original Intentionality** is grounded in **Phenomenal Consciousness**.
   - Any intentionality that is non-phenomenal (such as the "intentionality" of sentences in a book, computer databases, or unconscious cognitive tracking) is merely derivative or secondary, borrowing its significance from the phenomenal intentionality of conscious subjects.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: PIRP specifically uses the BIV to show that phenomenal intentionality can be isolated from the rest of the body.
- **(C) is incorrect**: This is the extreme externalist verdict, which PIRP presents as an intolerable reductio ad absurdum of tracking externalism.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Conflates original phenomenal intentionality with external machine tracking.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Terry Horgan and John Tienson, "The Intentionality of Phenomenology and the Phenomenology of Intentionality", in *Philosophy of Mind: Classical and Contemporary Readings*, ed. D. J. Chalmers (Oxford University Press, 2002), pp. 520–533; Terry Horgan, John Tienson, and George Graham, "Phenomenal Intentionality and the Brain in a Vat", in *The Externalist Challenge*, ed. Richard Schantz (de Gruyter, 2004), pp. 297–318; Uriah Kriegel (ed.), *Phenomenal Intentionality* (Oxford University Press, 2013).
