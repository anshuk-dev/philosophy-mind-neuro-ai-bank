### Question 1: Descartes, Princess Elisabeth, and the Causal Pairing Problem
**Domain**: Philosophy of Mind  
**Difficulty Tier**: Moderate / Systematic  
**Primary Thinkers**: René Descartes (*Meditations*, 1641), Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia (1643), Jaegwon Kim (1973, 2005)

Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia famously challenged René Descartes's substance dualism by asking how an unextended, immaterial soul could possibly exert physical force to move a material body (the Interaction Problem). Later, Jaegwon Kim sharpened this objection into the **"Causal Pairing Problem"**. What is the central dilemma posed by Kim's Causal Pairing Problem against Cartesian dualism?

- (A) Immaterial minds possess so much physical mass that they crush biological skull bones under gravitational acceleration.
- (B) The pairing problem asserts that Descartes was legally married to Princess Elisabeth under canon law.
- (C) The problem asserts that the pineal gland cannot be viewed under an optical light microscope.
- (D) Physical causation relies on spatial contiguity or spatial relations to pair a specific cause with its specific effect (e.g., why gun $A$ hitting target $A$ is the cause, rather than identical gun $B$); since immaterial minds lack spatial coordinates and spatial relations, Cartesian dualism has no metaphysical framework to explain why mind $M_1$ is causally paired with body $B_1$ rather than body $B_2$.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Immaterial minds are defined by Descartes as completely unextended and lacking physical mass.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Conflates metaphysical causal pairing with matrimonial union; Elisabeth was Descartes's intellectual correspondent, not spouse.
- **(C) is incorrect**: The pineal gland is visible upon anatomical dissection; the objection is metaphysical, not histological.
- **(D) is correct**: Jaegwon Kim (*Physicalism, or Something Near Enough*, 2005) proved that in physical space, if two identical guns fire simultaneously and two identical targets shatter, we explain which bullet caused which shattering by tracing spatial paths and spatial relations. But immaterial minds have no spatial location, no spatial orientation, and no spatial distance between them. Therefore, Cartesian dualism cannot account for the "pairing relation"—why my immaterial mind causes my arm to rise rather than your arm!

### Question 2: Hilary Putnam's Multiple Realizability vs. Type Identity
**Domain**: Philosophy of Mind  
**Difficulty Tier**: Basic / Foundational  
**Primary Thinkers**: Hilary Putnam (1967), J.J.C. Smart (1959)

In "Psychological Predicates" (1967), Hilary Putnam introduced the argument from **Multiple Realizability** against the Type Identity Theory of Smart and Place. Why does the Multiple Realizability thesis pose an existential challenge to reductive physicalism?

- (A) If a mental state type (such as pain) can be physically realized by radically different physical-chemical structures across different species (e.g., mammalian C-fibers, reptilian neural networks, an octopus's decentralized ganglia, or an alien silicon architecture), then pain cannot be identical to any single physical-chemical brain state type ($M \ne P$).
- (B) Putnam proved that octopuses and mammals do not have brains.
- (C) Multiple realizability proves that physical matter does not exist and that the universe is made entirely of Berkeleyan ideas.
- (D) The thesis asserts that human beings can clone multiple identical physical bodies simultaneously.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is correct**: Type Identity Theory asserts a strict 1-to-1 theoretical identity: $\text{Pain} = \text{C-fiber firing}$. Putnam argued that if an octopus or extraterrestrial feels pain without possessing mammalian C-fibers, pain cannot be identical to C-fiber firing. Mental states are functional/organizational properties, not identical to their specific physical realizers.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Octopuses possess complex nervous systems; Putnam's point depends precisely on their nervous system being anatomically different from human brains while realizing equivalent mental functions.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Multiple realizability is an argument within physicalist philosophy of mind (supporting non-reductive physicalism / functionalism); it is not subjective idealism.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Physical cloning is an irrelevant biological technology that does not define the functionalist thesis of multiple realization across diverse substrates.

### Question 3: The Libet Experiment and Alfred Mele's Critique
**Domain**: Neurophilosophy  
**Difficulty Tier**: Advance / Dialectical  
**Primary Thinkers**: Benjamin Libet (1983), Alfred Mele (*Free Will and Luck*, 2006)

Benjamin Libet's electroencephalogram (EEG) experiments on human subjects flexing their wrists demonstrated that the **Readiness Potential (RP)** begins approximately 350 to 550 milliseconds before the conscious awareness of the urge to act ($W$-time). While neuro-determinists claimed this disproved conscious free will, philosopher Alfred Mele refuted this conclusion. What is Mele's core conceptual refutation of Libet's interpretation?

- (A) Mele argued that human wrists are controlled entirely by spinal cord reflex arcs that bypass the brain.
- (B) Mele demonstrated that Benjamin Libet falsified his laboratory notebook timestamps.
- (C) Libet conflated an unconscious **proximal urge or preparatory inclination** with an actual conscious **intention or decision to act**; the Readiness Potential merely reflects non-binding motor preparation, whereas genuine executive intention formation can occur at or after $W$, including the conscious veto power.
- (D) Mele proved that EEG scalp electrodes generate false data whenever a participant blinks their eyes.

**Correct Answer**: **(C)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Voluntary wrist flexing is mediated by primary motor cortex and corticospinal tracts, not simple spinal reflex arcs.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Mele never accused Libet of academic dishonesty; he accepted Libet's empirical data while refuting his philosophical and conceptual interpretations.
- **(C) is correct**: Alfred Mele (*Effective Intentions*, 2009) demonstrated a fatal conceptual flaw in Libet's logic: an urge ($U$) is not a decision ($D$). The early rise of the Readiness Potential only shows that the brain is preparing a potential motor action; it does not commit the agent to acting. The conscious agent retains the executive capacity to veto or endorse the urge.
- **(D) is incorrect**: While ocular artifacts exist in electrophysiology, Libet used standard EOG filtering; Mele's critique is conceptual and epistemological, not an electrical artifact rejection.

### Question 4: Andy Clark & David Chalmers: Active Externalism and the Parity Principle
**Domain**: Philosophy of Mind / Cognitive Science  
**Difficulty Tier**: Moderate / Systematic  
**Primary Thinkers**: Andy Clark & David J. Chalmers (1998)

In their famous paper "The Extended Mind" (1998), Andy Clark and David Chalmers contrast Inga (who retrieves an address from biological biological memory) with Otto (who retrieves an address from a notebook due to Alzheimer's). What is their **"Parity Principle"**, and how does it establish active externalism?

- (A) Human brains and digital computers must be granted equal legal representation in democratic elections.
- (B) All biological organs in the human body possess equal cognitive intelligence, including the liver and kidneys.
- (C) If a part of the external world functions as a process which, were it done in the head, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is literally part of the cognitive process itself.
- (D) External objects can only be considered cognitive if they are physically implanted beneath human skin.

**Correct Answer**: **(C)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: The Parity Principle is an ontological and cognitive claim about the physical boundaries of mental processes, not a political theory of voting rights.
- **(B) is incorrect**: The authors never attribute cognitive function to metabolic organs; cognition is functional information processing.
- **(C) is correct**: The Parity Principle is Clark and Chalmers's central functionalist heuristic: we should not discriminate against external cognitive scaffolds purely because they sit outside the boundary of skin and skull. If Otto's notebook reliably, automatically, and constantly guides his actions just like Inga's biological memory, it constitutes an extended cognitive memory state.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Requiring an object to be implanted beneath the skin is the exact "intracranial chauvinism" that the Extended Mind thesis refutes.

### Question 5: John Searle's Chinese Room and the Syntax-Semantics Gap
**Domain**: Philosophy of AI  
**Difficulty Tier**: Basic / Foundational  
**Primary Thinkers**: John R. Searle (1980)

In "Minds, Brains, and Programs" (1980), John Searle formulated the Chinese Room thought experiment to attack Strong AI (computationalism). Searle distilled his argument into a famous triad of premises. Which of the following accurately captures Searle's foundational derivation?

- (A) 1. Silicon is non-conductive; 2. Brains are conductive; 3. Therefore, computers cannot execute programs.
- (B) 1. All symbols are physical tokens; 2. All minds are immaterial souls; 3. Therefore, physical tokens cannot produce immaterial souls.
- (C) 1. Chinese is an impossible language for machines; 2. English is the only logical language; 3. Therefore, computers cannot translate Chinese into English.
- (D) 1. Programs are purely formal (syntactic); 2. Human minds have mental contents (semantics); 3. Syntax alone is neither constitutive of nor sufficient for semantics; Therefore, programs are neither constitutive of nor sufficient for minds.

**Correct Answer**: **(D)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Silicon semiconductors conduct electricity; Searle's argument is about the inability of syntactic computation to bridge the semantic gap, not semiconductor physics.
- **(B) is incorrect**: Searle is a biological physicalist (*Biological Naturalism*); he explicitly rejects Cartesian immaterial souls.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Searle chose Chinese arbitrarily as an example of a language the agent in the room does not understand; he makes no chauvinistic claims about linguistic superiority.
- **(D) is correct**: Searle's formal deductive triad proves that manipulating symbols according to purely syntactic rules (like a computer following program instructions or a man in a room shuffling Chinese ideograms via an English rulebook) does not generate intrinsic intentionality or understanding of meaning (semantics).

### Question 6: Predictive Processing and Markov Blankets in Neurophilosophy
**Domain**: Neurophilosophy  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Karl Friston (2006, 2010), Jakob Hohwy (2013), Andy Clark (2013, 2016)

Under the **Free Energy Principle** and **Predictive Processing** paradigm (Friston, Hohwy, Clark), how does the mathematical formulation of a **Markov Blanket** establish the boundary of the cognitive agent and explain perceptual inference?

- (A) The Markov blanket proves that the external physical world does not exist, vindicating radical solipsism.
- (B) A Markov blanket partitions the state space into internal, external, sensory, and active states, rendering internal states conditionally independent of external states given sensory and active states; perception is the internal model's continuous minimization of variational free energy (prediction error) between predicted sensory states and incoming sensory signals.
- (C) The blanket refers to the outer myelin sheath surrounding peripheral nervous system axons.
- (D) A Markov blanket is a physical wool blanket placed over patients undergoing functional MRI scans to prevent hypothermia.

**Correct Answer**: **(B)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Predictive processing is a robust realist paradigm; it does not claim the world does not exist, but rather that the brain must indirectly infer the world's hidden causes through generative models.
- **(B) is correct**: In Pearl's and Friston's Bayesian formalism, the Markov blanket of a system consists of its sensory states (inputs from world) and active states (actions on world). The internal states (brain/mind) cannot directly access the external hidden causes of the world; they can only infer external states by minimizing prediction errors at the sensory boundary through Bayesian active inference.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Conflates the abstract statistical topology of Markov boundaries with the biological myelin sheath of Schwann cells.
- **(D) is incorrect**: A literal, humorous misinterpretation of "blanket".

### Question 7: Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and the Unfolding Argument
**Domain**: Philosophy of Mind  
**Difficulty Tier**: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers  
**Primary Thinkers**: Giulio Tononi (2004, 2016), Masafumi Oizumi (2014), Matthew Doerig, Andreas Schurger, & Michael Herzog (2019)

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) claims that consciousness is identical to integrated information ($\Phi^{\text{Max}}$) generated by a system's causal mechanisms. However, Doerig, Schurger, and Herzog (2019) formulated the **"Unfolding Argument"** against IIT. What is the central thesis of the Unfolding Argument?

- (A) For any complex recurrent neural network with high integrated information ($\Phi > 0$), there exists a mathematically input-output equivalent feedforward network with zero integrated information ($\Phi = 0$); since both networks generate identical behavioral functions across all possible tests, IIT must either make the untestable assertion that identical behaviors have radically different consciousness or decouple consciousness entirely from empirical science.
- (B) It demonstrates that $\Phi$ can only be calculated for biological brains that have undergone neurosurgery.
- (C) The unfolding argument shows that DNA molecules cannot unfold during protein transcription inside conscious neurons.
- (D) The argument proves that feedforward networks are conscious, while recurrent networks are unconscious.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is correct**: The Unfolding Theorem in automata theory proves that any recurrent network can be "unfolded" in time into an acyclic feedforward network with identical input-output behavior. In IIT, feedforward networks have $\Phi = 0$ (completely unconscious), while recurrent networks have $\Phi \gg 0$. This forces IIT into a dilemma: consciousness is either empirically unfalsifiable (since behavior cannot distinguish them) or IIT must accept that an entity exhibiting perfect human behavior, speech, and emotion could have zero consciousness.
- **(B) is incorrect**: $\Phi$ is a purely mathematical metric computed on transition probability matrices; it does not require neurosurgery.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Conflates mathematical graph unfolding with biochemical DNA unwinding.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Reverses IIT's premise; IIT attributes $\Phi > 0$ to recurrent structures and $\Phi = 0$ to feedforward ones.

### Question 8: The Symbol Grounding Problem and Stevan Harnad's Causal Transduction
**Domain**: Philosophy of AI  
**Difficulty Tier**: Moderate / Systematic  
**Primary Thinkers**: Stevan Harnad (1990)

In "The Symbol Grounding Problem" (1990), Stevan Harnad explored how symbolic computations acquire meaning. What is Harnad's famous **"Dictionary-Go-Round"** thought experiment, and how does he propose symbols must be grounded?

- (A) Symbols can only be grounded if they are memorized by rote learning in childhood primary education.
- (B) Searching for the meaning of a word in a dictionary written in a completely foreign language leads to an endless, ungrounded circular loop from meaningless symbol to meaningless symbol; symbols can only be grounded by bottom-up causal sensory-motor transducers and invariant categorical detectors that connect symbols directly to the external physical environment.
- (C) Harnad argues that symbols do not need grounding because syntax is identical to semantics.
- (D) Dictionaries are obsolete because digital search engines index words faster than human readers.

**Correct Answer**: **(B)**

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(A) is incorrect**: Childhood rote memorization does not address the metaphysical requirement of causal sensory grounding in cognitive architecture.
- **(B) is correct**: Harnad's Chinese/Chinese dictionary analogy demonstrates that symbolic manipulation alone cannot bridge meaning; one simply jumps from unknown ideogram to unknown ideogram forever. To ground symbols, Harnad proposed a hybrid cognitive architecture: analog sensory representations are transduced into non-symbolic invariant feature detectors, which then ground elementary symbols, from which higher-order symbolic thought can be built bottom-up.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Harnad strongly rejects the claim that syntax equals semantics; that was precisely the GOFAI dogma he challenged.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Digital search engines face the exact same semantic grounding crisis; Harnad's thought experiment is epistemological, not a practical comment on printed books vs. search engines.
