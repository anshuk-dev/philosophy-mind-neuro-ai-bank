## Co-Evolutionary Method, Eliminative Materialism, and Folk Psychology

### Question 1: Patricia Churchland and the "Co-Evolutionary" Research Ideology
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Basic / Foundational  
**Primary Thinkers**: Patricia Smith Churchland (1986)

In her foundational monograph *Neurophilosophy: Toward a Unified Science of the Mind-Brain* (1986), Patricia Smith Churchland argues that the traditional philosophical boundary between the philosophy of mind and empirical neuroscience must be replaced by a **co-evolutionary** methodology. What is the precise definition of "co-evolution" in Churchland’s neurophilosophical framework?

- (A) Top-down psychological theories and bottom-up neuroscientific theories must continuously mutually constrain, correct, and reshape each other until they converge into an integrated, unified theoretical paradigm.
- (B) Darwinian natural selection operates simultaneously on biological DNA and non-physical Cartesian soul-substances across evolutionary time.
- (C) Neuroscience must be entirely subordinated to folk psychological intuitions because human language evolved prior to modern neuroimaging.
- (D) Cognitive psychology should be abandoned immediately without waiting for any neurobiological evidence because all mental concepts are illusions.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
In *Neurophilosophy* (Chapter 9: "A Co-Evolutionary Research Strategy"), Patricia Churchland articulates the core methodological manifesto of the field. Rejecting both pure *a priori* armchair philosophy (which ignores neuroscience) and vulgar neuro-reductionism (which ignores high-level cognitive architecture), Churchland argues:
> "The co-evolutionary strategy suggests that top-down theories (such as cognitive psychology) and bottom-up theories (such as neurophysiology and neuroanatomy) should develop in tandem, each constraining, correcting, and informing the other... One does not wait for neuroscience to be finished before doing psychology, nor does one construct psychological models oblivious to the structural constraints of the brain."

In a co-evolutionary research programme:
1. *Bottom-up constraints*: Understanding the architectural realities of neural circuits (synaptic plasticity, laminar cortex organization, axonal transmission delays) prevents psychologists from postulating computationally impossible psychological mechanisms.
2. *Top-down guidance*: Cognitive models specify the behavioral capacities, visual computations, and cognitive functions that neuroscientists need to search for in neural wetware.
Over time, both vocabularies co-evolve until inter-theoretic reduction or elimination can take place.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B) is incorrect**: Churchland is an uncompromising physicalist; she vehemently rejects Cartesian soul-substances.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Churchland argues that folk psychology is deeply flawed and stagnant; she never subordinates neuroscience to everyday linguistic intuition.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Co-evolution is explicitly an *interactive*, phased transition, not an instantaneous blind dismissal of psychological modeling.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Patricia Smith Churchland, *Neurophilosophy: Toward a Unified Science of the Mind-Brain* (MIT Press, Bradford Books, 1986), Chapter 9: "A Co-Evolutionary Research Strategy", pp. 361–399.

### Question 2: Paul Churchland's Case for Eliminative Materialism
**Format**: Multi-Statement Evaluation / MSQ (Format C)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Basic / Foundational  
**Primary Thinkers**: Paul M. Churchland (1981)

In his landmark paper "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" (1981), Paul Churchland argues that our common-sense conceptual framework of the mind—known as **Folk Psychology** (which explains human behavior in terms of *beliefs*, *desires*, *intentions*, and *fears*)—is not an innate truth, but an empirical, explanatory theory that is fundamentally false. 

Consider the following arguments advanced by Churchland to justify the elimination (rather than smooth reduction) of folk psychology:
1. *The Argument from Widespread Explanatory Failure*: Folk psychology leaves the vast majority of mental phenomena completely unexplained—including the nature of sleep, dreams, memory consolidation, mental illness, learning mechanics, and perceptual illusions.
2. *The Argument from Historical Stagnation*: Folk psychology has not made any significant theoretical progress in 2,500 years; Aristotle or Sophocles understood human behavior using the exact same propositional attitude framework we use today.
3. *The Argument from Historical Precedent*: Just as past false theories were not smoothly reduced but outright eliminated—such as the phlogiston theory of combustion (replaced by oxygen chemistry), the caloric theory of heat (replaced by thermodynamics), and the witch theory of psychosis (replaced by neuropathology)—so propositional attitudes will be eliminated by mature cognitive neuroscience.

Which of the statements given above are genuine tenets of Churchland’s argument for Eliminative Materialism?
- (A) 1, 2, and 3
- (B) 1 and 2 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1 only

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
Paul Churchland’s 1981 paper is the definitive statement of **Eliminative Materialism**. Churchland defines eliminativism as:
> "The thesis that our common-sense conception of psychological phenomena constitutes a radically false theory, a theory so fundamentally defective that both the principles and the ontology of that theory will eventually be displaced, rather than smoothly reduced, by completed neuroscience."

He structures his case around three foundational arguments:
1. *Explanatory Poverty (Statement 1)*: Folk psychology is remarkably narrow. It cannot explain the physiological necessity of sleep, the mechanism of dreaming, the retrieval of memories, the etiology of schizophrenia, or developmental differences in learning.
2. *Theoretical Stagnation (Statement 2)*: A healthy scientific research programme expands its predictive domain and develops new explanatory laws. Folk psychology, by contrast, is stagnant: the explanation of human action in Homer or Sophocles in terms of beliefs, passions, and desires is essentially identical to our common-sense discourse today. It is a degenerating research programme (in Imre Lakatos’s sense).
3. *Coherence with Physical Science & Historical Precedent (Statement 3)*: When a theoretical ontology cannot be matched to the categories of the underlying science, it is *eliminated*, not reduced:
   - *Phlogiston* was not reduced to oxygen; scientists realized there is no such entity as phlogiston.
   - *Caloric fluid* was eliminated once heat was recognized as molecular kinetic energy.
   - *Witches* were not reduced to neurotics; the category was discarded.
Similarly, Churchland argues that beliefs and desires will not map neatly onto neural networks; they will be eliminated as obsolete folk fictions.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B), (C), and (D) are incorrect**: All three arguments (Explanatory Deficits, Stagnation, and Historical Elimination) are canonical, foundational pillars of Churchland's 1981 thesis.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Paul M. Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes", *Journal of Philosophy* 78(2) (1981): 67–90.

### Question 3: Split-Brain Commissurotomy and Thomas Nagel's Puzzle
**Format**: Conceptual Multiple-Choice (Format A)  
**Difficulty Tier**: Basic / Foundational  
**Primary Thinkers**: Roger Sperry (1968), Michael Gazzaniga (1970), Thomas Nagel (1971)

Following surgical resection of the corpus callosum (commissurotomy) to treat intractable epilepsy, split-brain patients were tested by Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga using tachistoscopic visual presentation (flashing stimuli exclusively to the left or right visual hemifield). In "Brain Bisection and the Unity of Consciousness" (1971), what philosophical puzzle does Thomas Nagel deduce regarding the number of conscious minds in a split-brain patient?

- (A) None of the five possible hypotheses—(1) one unified mind, (2) two separate minds, (3) one mind with an unconscious sub-system, (4) one normal mind and one robotic system, or (5) a fluctuating fraction of a mind—can be coherently reconciled with our standard concept of an individual conscious subject, proving that our everyday concept of a unified "self" breaks down when applied to a surgically bisected brain.
- (B) Split-brain patients conclusively prove that Descartes was correct: the soul resides exclusively in the right hemisphere while the body is governed by the left hemisphere.
- (C) The experiment proves that the left hemisphere alone is conscious because only the left hemisphere has syntactic Broca's speech capabilities.
- (D) The surgery demonstrates that conscious experience is completely duplicated, creating two immortal metaphysical souls residing in a single skull.

**Correct Answer**: **(A)**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration:
When sensory information is flashed to the left visual field (routed to the right hemisphere), a split-brain patient cannot verbally name the object (since speech is localized in the left hemisphere), but their left hand (controlled by the right hemisphere) can effortlessly reach behind a screen, locate the object by touch, and draw it. 

In "Brain Bisection and the Unity of Consciousness" (1971), Thomas Nagel analyzes the five conceivable interpretations of this phenomenon:
1. *Hypothesis 1*: The patient has a single, normal unified mind, but disconnected pathways. (Refuted because the two hemispheres can simultaneously perform conflicting voluntary intentional tasks).
2. *Hypothesis 2*: The patient has two completely independent conscious minds—a speaking left-mind and a mute right-mind. (Refuted because outside the laboratory, in ordinary daily life, the patient exhibits integrated, unified, non-conflicting personal behavior).
3. *Hypothesis 3*: The patient has one conscious mind (the left hemisphere) and an unconscious automaton (the right hemisphere). (Refuted because the right hemisphere exhibits learning, emotion, intention, and perceptual discrimination).
4. *Hypothesis 4*: The patient has one normal mind that switches back and forth. (Refuted by simultaneous dual-task execution).
5. *Hypothesis 5*: The mind is a combination of 1.5 minds. (Lacks any coherent semantic meaning).

Nagel concludes that **the ordinary common-sense concept of a single, indivisible conscious subject breaks down**:
> "The idea of a single subject of experience cannot be applied to the split-brain patient. But neither can the idea of two subjects. The radical conclusion is that the concept of an individual subject of consciousness is an illusion that cannot withstand physical dissection of the anatomical substrate."

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **(B) is incorrect**: Nagel rejects Cartesian dualism; Descartes claimed the soul is indivisible (*res cogitans* has no parts), which commissurotomy directly refutes.
- **(C) is incorrect**: Gazzaniga and Sperry proved that the mute right hemisphere is undeniably conscious, possesses emotional reactions, and comprehends complex language.
- **(D) is incorrect**: Nagel’s conclusion is physicalist, skeptical, and bundle-theoretic; he does not postulate metaphysical immortal souls.

**Primary Textual Reference**: Thomas Nagel, "Brain Bisection and the Unity of Consciousness", *Synthese* 22(3/4) (1971): 396–413.
