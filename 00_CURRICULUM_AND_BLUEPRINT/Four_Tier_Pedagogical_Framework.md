# The Four-Tier Difficulty Framework & Authoring Standard
## Rigorous Pedagogical Specification for Mind, Neurophilosophy & AI Question Banks

This document codifies the exact structural, conceptual, and distractor criteria governing questions across the **Four Tiers of Difficulty**. Every question in this repository strictly adheres to this matrix.

---

### Tier Matrix & Evaluative Criteria

| Dimension | Tier 1: Basic / Foundational | Tier 2: Moderate / Systematic | Tier 3: Advance / Dialectical | Tier 4: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cognitive Objective** | Core definitions, classic thought experiments, primary attribution | Formal argumentation, systematic counter-examples, standard mechanics | Edge cases, second-generation critiques, multi-premise tensions | Faculty-interview depth, fine boundary conditions, formal proofs |
| **Philosophical Depth** | Direct thesis formulation (e.g., Turing test, Chinese room, DN model) | Inter-theoretic friction (e.g., Block's China Brain vs Functionalism) | Systemic dilemmas (e.g., Kim's causal exclusion, $P \land \neg Q$ 2D semantics) | Frontier debate (e.g., Mechanistic interpretability circuits, Rashomon sets) |
| **Distractor Architecture** | Clear historical or definitional alternatives | Plausible misreadings of classical formulations | Subtle conceptual conflations from neighboring traditions | Structurally sophisticated counter-positions with precise flaws diagnosed |
| **Primary References** | Foundational papers (Turing 1950, Searle 1980, Nagel 1974) | Canonical treatises (Churchland 1986, Fodor 1988, Salmon 1984) | Advanced monographs (Kim 1998, Chalmers 1996, Woodward 2003) | Contemporary frontier publications (Friston 2010, Rudin 2019, Bostrom 2014) |

---
