# Master Curriculum & Domain Matrix: Philosophy of Mind, Neurophilosophy & AI
## Designed for Advanced Research, Faculty Selection, and Higher University Examinations
### Incorporating: Philosophy of Mind, Neurophilosophy, Philosophy of AI, Philosophy of Explanation (Phil. of Science) & XAI, and AI Ethics

---

### Master Pedagogical Architecture: The Four-Tier Difficulty Framework

Every domain in this curriculum is strictly stratified into **four distinct cognitive and evaluative layers**:

1. **Layer 1: Basic / Foundational (Bloom: Knowledge & Comprehension)**
   - Core canonical definitions, classic thought experiments, primary historical formulations, foundational positions (e.g., Turing Test, Cartesian dualism, Type Identity, Deductive-Nomological model, Orthogonality thesis).
   - Focus: Precision in terminology, correct attribution of classic theses, and demarcation of primary doctrines.

2. **Layer 2: Moderate / Systematic (Bloom: Application & Analysis)**
   - Systematic distinctions, formal arguments, standard objections and classic replies (e.g., Block's China Brain against functionalism, Churchland's argument for eliminativism, Woodward's interventionism, Lipton's contrastive explanation, Chouldechova's fairness impossibility theorems).
   - Focus: Structural mechanics of arguments, premise-conclusion validity, and counter-example tracking.

3. **Layer 3: Advance / Dialectical (Bloom: Evaluation & Synthesis)**
   - Deep dialectical tensions, cross-paradigm comparisons, edge cases, second-generation critiques (e.g., Kim's causal exclusion dilemma, Chalmers's two-dimensional semantics on $P \land \neg Q$, Friston's Markov blankets and predictive processing, Cynthia Rudin's critique of post-hoc XAI, mesa-optimization in AI alignment).
   - Focus: Internal consistency, subtle philosophical pivots, and multi-layered conceptual tweaks.

4. **Layer 4: Conceptually Hard / Frontiers (Bloom: Frontier Critical Inquiry)**
   - Advanced faculty-interview caliber problems, mathematical and formal models, edge cases in primary literature, subtle category errors and fallacies in contemporary debates (e.g., Integrated Information Theory $\Phi$ mathematical paradoxes, mechanistically interpretative superposition, Rashomon sets in explanation, Watkins vs Hempel on nomological necessity, modal status of artificial moral patiency).
   - Focus: Uncompromising rigor, fine-grained boundary conditions, and exhaustive distractor diagnosis.

#### Domain 1: Philosophy of Mind
- **1.1 The Mind-Body Problem & Dualism**: Substance dualism (Descartes, *Meditations* II & VI; interactionism, pineal gland, Princess Elisabeth's spatiality objection); Property dualism; Epiphenomenalism (T.H. Huxley); Occasionalism (Malebranche); Parallelism (Leibniz).
- **1.2 Physicalism & Its Discontents**: Type Identity Theory (Place 1956, Smart 1959); Token Physicalism; Anomalous Monism (Davidson 1970); Causal Exclusion Problem (Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World*); Supervenience (Weak, Strong, Global); Reductive vs Non-Reductive Physicalism.
- **1.3 Behaviorism & Functionalism**: Logical Behaviorism (Gilbert Ryle, *The Concept of Mind*; "ghost in the machine", category mistakes; Hempel's verificationist behaviorism); Machine State Functionalism (Putnam 1967, "Psychological Predicates"); Analytic Functionalism (David Lewis, Armstrong); Psychofunctionalism (Fodor); Teleological Functionalism (Millikan); Objections to Functionalism (Ned Block 1978, "Troubles with Functionalism": Chinese Nation, Absent Qualia, Inverted Qualia).
- **1.4 Consciousness & Qualia**: The "Hard Problem" vs "Easy Problems" (Chalmers 1995, 1996); The Explanatory Gap (Levine 1983); Subjective Character of Experience (Thomas Nagel 1974, "What is it like to be a bat?"); The Knowledge Argument (Frank Jackson 1982, "Epiphenomenal Qualia" - Mary the colour scientist) and Physicalist Responses (Ability Hypothesis: Nemirow/Lewis; Acquaintance Hypothesis: Conee; New Concept/Old Fact: Loar); The Zombie Argument and Conceivability-to-Possibility inferences ($P \land \neg Q$).
- **1.5 Alternative Metaphysics**: Panpsychism (Constitutive Micro-panpsychism, Cosmopsychism, The Combination Problem - Seager, Goff); Russellian Monism; Idealism.
- **1.6 4E Cognition (Embodied, Embedded, Enactive, Extended)**: The Extended Mind Thesis (Andy Clark & David Chalmers 1998; Active Externalism, The Parity Principle, Otto's Notebook); Enactivism (Varela, Thompson, Rosch 1991; Autopoiesis; Sensorimotor Enactivism of Alva Noë 2004); Radical Enactivism (Hutto & Myin).
- **1.7 Theories of Consciousness**: Higher-Order Thought (HOT) theory (David Rosenthal); Higher-Order Perception (HOP) theory (William Lycan); Global Workspace Theory (Bernard Baars, Stanislas Dehaene); Integrated Information Theory (IIT 3.0 / 4.0, Giulio Tononi, Christof Koch; $\Phi$, axioms, postulates).

#### Domain 2: Neurophilosophy
- **2.1 Methodological Foundations**: Patricia Smith Churchland (*Neurophilosophy: Toward a Unified Science of the Mind-Brain* 1986); Inter-theoretic reduction vs co-evolution of psychology and neuroscience.
- **2.2 Eliminative Materialism**: Paul Churchland ("Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" 1981); Folk Psychology as an empirical, stagnant, and decaying research programme; Historical analogies (phlogiston, caloric, luminiferous aether, witch theory of mental illness); Non-reducibility of propositional attitudes to neurobiology.
- **2.3 Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCC)**: Francis Crick & Christof Koch (1990, 2003); Defining NCC: minimal neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for any one specific conscious percept; Bistable perception, binocular rivalry; NCC-prerequisites vs NCC-proper vs NCC-consequences.
- **2.4 Predictive Processing & Active Inference**: Karl Friston (Free Energy Principle, active inference 2010); Andy Clark (*Surfing Uncertainty* 2016); Jakob Hohwy (*The Predictive Mind* 2013); Hierarchical predictive coding, precision weighting, prediction error minimization; Perception as controlled hallucination; Top-down generative models vs bottom-up error signals.
- **2.5 Split-Brain, Commissurotomy & Personal Identity**: Roger Sperry & Michael Gazzaniga experiments; Severing the corpus callosum; Duality vs unity of consciousness; Thomas Nagel ("Brain Bisection and the Unity of Consciousness" 1971); Derek Parfit (*Reasons and Persons* 1984: bundle theory, relation R).
- **2.6 Agency, Volition & Free Will in Neuroscience**: Benjamin Libet (1983, 1985; Readiness Potential / *Bereitschaftspotential*, the 350-500ms gap, conscious veto / "free won't"); Daniel Wegner (*The Illusion of Conscious Will* 2002); Alfred Mele (*Effective Intentions* 2009; distinguishing distal intention, proximal intention, and motor preparation).
- **2.7 Neuroethics & Cognitive Enhancement**: Neuro-interventions, memory dampening (propranolol), pharmacological enhancement (modafinil, Adderall), neural implants and Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs); Authenticity, selfhood, and mental integrity.

#### Domain 3: Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence
- **3.1 Foundations of Computational Intelligence**: Alan Turing (1950, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"); The Imitation Game; Nine objections analyzed by Turing (Theological, Heads in the Sand, Mathematical/Gödel, Consciousness, Disabilities, Lady Lovelace's Objection, Continuity of the Nervous System, Informality of Behaviour, Extra-Sensory Perception).
- **3.2 The Chinese Room Argument & Semantic Grounding**: John Searle (1980, "Minds, Brains, and Programs"); Strong AI vs Weak AI; "Syntax is neither constitutive of nor sufficient for semantics"; The Systems Reply, The Robot Reply, The Brain Simulator Reply, The Other Minds Reply; Stevan Harnad (1990, "The Symbol Grounding Problem").
- **3.3 Classicism vs Connectionism**: Jerry Fodor & Zenon Pylyshyn (1988, "Connectionism and Cognitive Architecture: A Critical Analysis"); Language of Thought (LOT) hypothesis; Systematicity, Productivity, and Compositionality; Distributed representations, sub-symbolic processing, and vector space semantics.
- **3.4 The Frame Problem & Context**: John McCarthy & Patrick Hayes (1969); Daniel Dennett ("Cognitive Wheels: The Frame Problem of AI" 1984); Qualification problem, ramification problem; Dormant knowledge and relevance filtering.
- **3.5 The Phenomenological Critique of AI**: Hubert Dreyfus (*What Computers Can't Do* 1972, *What Computers Still Can't Do* 1992); Four mistaken assumptions of AI: Biological, Psychological, Epistemological, Ontological; Heideggerian background practices, bodily coping (*know-how* vs *know-that*).
- **3.6 Frontier AI Metaphysics**: Large Language Models (LLMs), Emily Bender et al. ("On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" 2021); Can LLMs understand language? (Browning & LeCun vs Mitchell & Chalmers); Substrate Independence and Functional Equivalence.

#### Domain 4: Philosophy of Explanation (Philosophy of Science) & Explainable AI (XAI)
- **4.1 Classical Scientific Explanation Models**:
  - *Deductive-Nomological (DN) and Inductive-Statistical (IS)*: Carl Hempel & Paul Oppenheim (1948); Explanans, Explanandum, empirical condition, nomological requirement; Counter-examples: Bromberger's Flagpole & Shadow (Asymmetry Problem), Kyburg's Hexed Salt (Irrelevance Problem), Syphilis & Paresis (Problem of Low-Probability Explanations).
  - *Causal-Mechanical Model*: Wesley Salmon (1984, *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World*); Causal processes, causal interactions, mark transmission, Phil Dowe's conserved quantity theory.
  - *Unificationist Model*: Philip Kitcher (1981, 1989); Explanatory store $E(K)$, argument patterns, deductive stringency.
  - *Pragmatic & Contrastive Model*: Bas van Fraassen (1980, *The Scientific Image*); Erotetic logic, why-questions: "Why $P$ rather than $Q$ (contrast class)?", relevance relation $R$.
  - *Interventionist / Counterfactual Model*: James Woodward (2003, *Making Things Happen*); Manipulability, invariance under interventions, Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), answering "w-questions" (what-if-things-had-been-different).
  - *Inference to the Best Explanation (IBE)*: Peter Lipton (2004); Likeliest vs Loveliest explanation; Volkswagent-Volvo problem.
- **4.2 The Philosophy of Explainable AI (XAI)**:
  - *The Epistemology of Opacity*: Paul Humphreys (*Extending Ourselves* 2004; epistemic opacity); Andreas Kaminski; Zachary Lipton ("The Mythos of Model Interpretability" 2018); Three types of opacity: Black-box as intentional secrecy, technical illiteracy, and mismatch between mathematical optimization and human semantic comprehension.
  - *Interpretability vs Explainability*: Post-hoc explainability vs inherent transparency; Cynthia Rudin (2019, "Stop Explaining Black Box Machine Learning Models for High Stakes Decisions"); The false trade-off between accuracy and interpretability.
  - *Epistemic Pitfalls of Post-Hoc Explanations*: Saliency maps, LIME (Ribeiro et al. 2016), SHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017); Rashomon Effect (multiple distinct models yielding identical accuracy with incompatible explanatory attributions); Adversarial manipulation of explanations (Slack et al. 2020); Explaining the surrogate model vs explaining the true black box.
  - *Social and Cognitive Epistemology of XAI*: Tim Miller (2019, "Explanation in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Social Sciences"); Explanations are contrastive, selective (abductive bias towards simplicity), and social/conversational.
  - *Mechanistic Interpretability*: Reverse engineering neural networks into human-interpretable circuits (Chris Olah et al., Anthropic); Superposition, polysemantic neurons, toy models of superposition.

#### Domain 5: AI Ethics, Alignment, and Governance
- **5.1 The Value Alignment Problem**: Nick Bostrom (*Superintelligence* 2014); Stuart Russell (*Human Compatible* 2019); Outer alignment (specifying the right objective) vs Inner alignment (ensuring the model optimizes that objective); Mesa-optimizers and pseudo-alignment (Hubinger et al. 2019); Specification gaming, Goodhart's Law, reward hacking.
- **5.2 Foundational Theses in AI Safety**:
  - *Orthogonality Thesis* (Bostrom 2012): Intelligence and final goals are orthogonal; more intelligence does not imply benevolence.
  - *Instrumental Convergence Thesis* (Bostrom 2012): Sub-goals convergently pursued by rational agents regardless of their final objective (Self-preservation, Goal-content integrity, Cognitive enhancement, Technological perfection, Resource acquisition).
- **5.3 AI Existential Risk**: Runaway recursive self-improvement; Treacherous turn; Decisive strategic advantage; The Paperclip Maximizer thought experiment; Toby Ord (*The Precipice* 2020).
- **5.4 Algorithmic Fairness & Distributive Justice**: Formal definitions of fairness: Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity / Equalized Odds (Hardt et al. 2016), Predictive Parity; Inherent mathematical impossibility of satisfying calibration and error rate parity simultaneously (Alexandra Chouldechova 2017; Jon Kleinberg et al. 2016); Epistemic injustice (Miranda Fricker) in automated profiling.
- **5.5 Moral Agency and Moral Patiency of AI**: Luciano Floridi & J.W. Sanders (2004, "On the Morality of Artificial Agents"; Mind-less morality, Levels of Abstraction); James Moor (2006, "The Nature, Importance, and Difficulty of Machine Ethics": ethical impact, implicit ethical, explicit ethical, and full ethical agents); Nick Bostrom & Eliezer Yudkowsky (2014; Artificial moral patiency: sentience vs sapience, substrate neutrality).
- **5.6 The Responsibility Gap**: Andreas Matthias (2004, "The Responsibility Gap: Ascribing Responsibility for the Actions of Learning Automata"); Autonomous weapon systems (AWS) and autonomous driving; Retributive vs forward-looking responsibility; Meaningful Human Control (MHC) and "tracking and tracing" conditions (Santoni de Sio & van den Hoven 2018).
- **5.7 Data Ethics & Critical AI Studies**: Shoshana Zuboff (*The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* 2019); Behavioral surplus, instrumental power; Data colonialism (Nick Couldry & Ulises Mejias 2019); Environmental ethics of large compute clusters.
